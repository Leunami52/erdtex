+++
id = "ER-23"
title = "Between two weak entity types, the dependent one must be chosen"
kind = "warning"
applies = ["weak relationship type", "weak entity type", "line"]
see = ["ER-21", "ER-24"]
+++
**Rule.** Where a weak relationship type joins two weak entity types, exactly one of its two lines
**should** be double: the one to the entity type that depends on the other.

**What the program does.** A warning, *"this relationship joins two weak entities, A and B: exactly one
link has to be double-lined, the one to the entity that depends on the other. Choose it under
Dependent"*, while no line or both lines are double. The relationship type's *Element* pane has the field
*Dependent*, which sets the double line.

- While drawing, the second weak entity type leaves both lines single and selects the relationship, since
  the program can't guess the direction.
- Without a decision, the relational schema falls back on the cardinalities to find the owner, and says so.

**Why.** A seat depends on its room, which depends on its building: the room is weak, but between room
and seat it is the owner. Only the double line can say which of two weak entity types depends on which;
a guess would give a schema that looks right and isn't.

**Examples.**

```erd violates ER-23
\entity{building}(0,0){Building}
\attributes[anchor=west]{building}{*Name}
\relationship[dependency=Id]{contains}(4,0){contains}
\entity[weak]{room}(8,0){Room}
\attributes{room}{*Number}
\link{building}{contains}{1}
\link[double]{contains}{room}{n}
\relationship[dependency=Id]{has}(8,-3){has}
\entity[weak]{seat}(8,-6){Seat}
\attributes{seat}{*Number}
\link{room}{has}{1}
\link{has}{seat}{n}
```

