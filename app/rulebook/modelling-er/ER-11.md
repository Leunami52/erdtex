+++
id = "ER-11"
title = "Every line between an entity type and a relationship type should have a cardinality"
kind = "hint"
applies = ["line", "entity type", "relationship type"]
see = ["NOT-1", "ER-12", "ER-13"]
+++
**Rule.** Every line between an entity type and a relationship type **should** say its cardinality.

**What the program does.** A hint, *"no cardinality on this entity–relationship edge"*, at a line
without a label between an entity type (weak or not) and a relationship type (weak or not). The
*Element* pane of the line offers the cardinalities of the diagram's notation in a picker, with what the
chosen one means.

- Lines of a PART_OF, of a generalisation (IS_A, CAN_BE) and of Chen attributes need none and get no hint.
- A line through junction points counts as one: the hint is given at its piece at the entity type, and
  the cardinality may stand on any of its pieces.

**Why.** Without cardinalities a relationship says that entities are related, but not how many of
them: whether a student attends one course or many, and whether every student has to. The relational
schema needs exactly that to decide where foreign keys go.

**Examples.**

```erd violates ER-11
\entity{student}(0,0){Student}
\attributes[anchor=west]{student}{*Student Id}
\relationship{attends}(4,0){attends}
\entity{course}(8,0){Course}
\attributes{course}{*Course Id}
\link{student}{attends}
\link{attends}{course}{cn}
```

```erd violates ER-11
\entity{student}(0,0){Student}
\attributes[anchor=west]{student}{*Student Id}
\point{p}(2,1)
\relationship{attends}(4,0){attends}
\entity{course}(8,0){Course}
\attributes{course}{*Course Id}
\link{student}{p}
\link{p}{attends}
\link{attends}{course}{cn}
```
