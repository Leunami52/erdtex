+++
id = "ER-33"
title = "A generalisation should say whether it is complete and disjoint"
kind = "warning"
applies = ["IS_A", "CAN_BE"]
see = ["NOT-1", "ER-1"]
+++
**Rule.** Every generalisation (IS_A or CAN_BE) **should** say both whether it is *complete* or
*incomplete*, and whether it is *disjoint* or *overlapping*.

**What the program does.** A warning, *"this generalisation does not say whether it is disjoint or
overlapping and complete or incomplete"* (or only the part that is missing). The *Element* pane of the
generalisation sets both (*Coverage*, *Overlap*).

| Letter | Means |
|---|---|
| `c` | complete: every entity of the supertype is in at least one subtype |
| `i` | incomplete: some entities of the supertype are in no subtype |
| `d` | disjoint: an entity is in at most one subtype |
| `o` | overlapping: an entity may be in several subtypes |

The file keeps the letters (`cd`, `co`, `id`, `io`), whatever the diagram shows. Written-out words are
understood as well: *complete* (or *total*), *incomplete* (or *partial*), *disjoint*, *overlapping*.

**Why.** The two say different things the database has to know: whether a person who is neither
employee nor student can exist (complete or not), and whether one can be both (disjoint or not). They
also decide the relational schema's options, and whether the supertype needs a key of its own (ER-1).

**Examples.**

```erd violates ER-33
\entity{person}(0,0){Person}
\attributes{person}{*SSN, Name}
\isa{g}(0,-2){person}{c}
\entity{employee}(-2,-4){Employee}
\entity{student}(2,-4){Student}
\link{employee}{g}
\link{student}{g}
```

```erd violates ER-33
\entity{person}(0,0){Person}
\attributes{person}{*SSN, Name}
\isa{g}(0,-2){person}{}
\entity{employee}(-2,-4){Employee}
\entity{student}(2,-4){Student}
\link{employee}{g}
\link{student}{g}
```

```erd follows
\entity{person}(0,0){Person}
\attributes{person}{*SSN, Name}
\isa{g}(0,-2){person}{io}
\entity{employee}(-2,-4){Employee}
\entity{student}(2,-4){Student}
\link{employee}{g}
\link{student}{g}
```
