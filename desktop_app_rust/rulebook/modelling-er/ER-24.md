+++
id = "ER-24"
title = "A weak entity type depends on exactly one owner"
kind = "warning"
applies = ["weak relationship type", "weak entity type", "line"]
see = ["NOT-1", "ER-19", "ER-26"]
+++
**Rule.** The cardinalities of a weak relationship type **should** allow each dependent entity at most
one owner.

**What the program does.** A warning, *"'Room' depends on exactly one 'Building', so its cardinality
here can't allow more than one"*, at the relationship type, when its labels allow a dependent more than
one owner:

| Notation | Where the owners of one dependent are counted | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| n-notation | at the **owner's** line | `1`, `c` |
| min-max | at the **dependent's** line | `(1,1)`, `(0,1)` |

- It applies to weak relationship types between two different entity types; one between an entity type
  and itself is ER-26 and ER-27.
- Between two weak entity types it applies to the dependent one (ER-23).
- A missing or unreadable label isn't reported here (ER-11, ER-13).

**Why.** A weak entity takes its identity or its existence from one owner: a room is a room of one
building. With several owners it would be undecided whose key it takes and whose deletion deletes it.

**Examples.**

```erd violates ER-24
\entity{building}(0,0){Building}
\attributes[anchor=west]{building}{*Name}
\relationship[dependency=Id]{contains}(4,0){contains}
\entity[weak]{room}(8,0){Room}
\attributes{room}{*Number}
\link{building}{contains}{n}
\link[double]{contains}{room}{n}
```

```erd violates ER-24
\setup{card notation=minmax}
\entity{building}(0,0){Building}
\attributes[anchor=west]{building}{*Name}
\relationship[dependency=Id]{contains}(4,0){contains}
\entity[weak]{room}(8,0){Room}
\attributes{room}{*Number}
\link{building}{contains}{(1,*)}
\link[double]{contains}{room}{(1,*)}
```
